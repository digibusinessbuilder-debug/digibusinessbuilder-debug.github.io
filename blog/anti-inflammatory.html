Anti-Inflammatory Lifestyle: 7 Daily Habits

Medically reviewed by Fawad Ahmed, MD on April 5, 2025

Published April 5, 2025 · Updated October 15, 2026

A comprehensive approach to reducing inflammation and supporting overall health through natural lifestyle interventions — sleep, light therapy, nature connection, movement, and diet.

A comprehensive approach to reducing inflammation and supporting overall health through natural lifestyle interventions.

1. Sleep

Quality sleep is essential for reducing inflammation and supporting the body's natural healing processes.

Routine

Sleep and wake at the same time daily, even on weekends.

Wind-down

Limit screens 1 hour before bed. Dim or reduce blue light after 6pm. Use warm indirect lights or candlelight.

Sleep environment

Keep the room cool (65–68°F), dark, and quiet. Use blackout curtains.

Bonus

Use a weighted blanket to calm the nervous system.

Supplements

Consider magnesium glycinate 400–800 mg at bedtime or tart cherry juice (natural melatonin).

Breathing

Practice 4-7-8 breathing before sleep to reduce cortisol.

What to avoid

Alcohol and caffeine 6 hours before bed.

2. Red light therapy

Red light therapy uses visible red light wavelengths, typically between 630 and 700 nanometers (nm).

Recommended use

  • Frequency: 3–5 times per week
  • Duration: 10–20 minutes per session
  • Time of Day: Morning or early evening (avoid close to bedtime if it energizes you)

Reported benefits

  • Reduces inflammation and joint pain (e.g., arthritis)
  • Boosts collagen production (anti-aging, skin healing)
  • Improves muscle recovery and reduces soreness
  • Supports thyroid function

Tip: Use a device with a combination of near-infrared (NIR) and red light.

3. Time in nature

Daily sun exposure

15–20 minutes, ideally during the first hour of the day (regulates circadian rhythm).

Forest bathing

Spend time in green spaces 2–3 times a week (lowers cortisol, boosts immunity).

Earthing

Walk barefoot on grass, soil, or sand for 15 minutes daily to absorb negative ions (reduces inflammation and stress).

Bonus

Listen to natural sounds (birds, wind) to relax the nervous system.

4. Grounding mats

Earthing indoors with grounding mats can provide many of the same benefits as direct contact with the earth.

How to use them

Place under your feet while working or sleeping (use for 30 minutes to overnight).

Reported effects

Reduces inflammation by neutralizing free radicals; lowers cortisol levels and improves sleep; reduces pain from conditions like arthritis or migraines.

Tip: Use a grounding mat made from conductive materials like carbon or silver threads.

5. Yoga

Specific yoga poses can help reduce inflammation and pain in different parts of the body.

Poses for inflammation and joint pain

Child's pose (Balasana)

Reduces lower back inflammation and calms the mind. Hold for 2 minutes.

Legs-up-the-wall (Viparita Karani)

Reduces swelling in legs and feet. Hold for 5 minutes.

Cat-cow stretch

Improves spinal flexibility and reduces inflammation. Do 10 rounds.

6. Dry brushing

Dry brushing supports lymphatic drainage and helps reduce inflammation throughout the body.

How, when and for how long

How

Use a natural bristle brush, starting at your feet and brushing upwards toward your heart, in long sweeping motions.

When

Before showering, 2–3 times per week.

Duration

3–5 minutes.

Tip

Follow with a cold shower for extra anti-inflammatory benefits.

Benefits

Stimulates lymphatic drainage (reduces inflammation), exfoliates dead skin, boosts circulation.

7. Anti-inflammatory diet

What you eat has a profound impact on inflammation levels in your body.

Foods to eat more of

Fatty Fish (salmon, sardines)

Omega-3s fight inflammation

Leafy Greens (spinach, kale)

Rich in antioxidants

Berries (blueberries, raspberries)

High in polyphenols

Turmeric with Black Pepper

Potent anti-inflammatory duo

Fermented Foods (kimchi, sauerkraut)

Supports gut health

Bone Broth

Supports joints and reduces gut inflammation

Healthy Fats

Avocados, olive oil, chia seeds, walnuts

Herbs & Spices

Ginger, garlic, rosemary, and cinnamon

Hydration

2–3 liters of water daily (add lemon or cucumber for extra antioxidants)

Foods to limit

  • Processed foods — Chips, sweets, refined grains
  • Vegetable oils — Canola, soybean oil
  • Sugary beverages
  • Excess alcohol
  • Red meat — Limit to grass-fed and three times a week

More lifestyle habits

Cold showers or ice baths

1–2 minutes post-workout to reduce muscle inflammation

Intermittent fasting

14–16 hours overnight fast (promotes cellular repair and reduces inflammation)

Gratitude practice

Reduces stress-related inflammation markers (e.g., cortisol)
