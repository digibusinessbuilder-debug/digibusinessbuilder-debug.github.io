Is Functional Medicine Right for Me?

Jordan Casey, MS-CNFM · Master of Science in Clinical Nutrition and Functional Medicine, University of Western States

Medically reviewed by Fawad Ahmed, MD on November 29, 2025

Published November 29, 2025 · Updated October 15, 2026

If you've bounced between specialists and been told your labs are 'normal' even though you don't feel well, functional medicine may be the next step — here's how to know if it's a fit.

Book a Consultation

If you've bounced between specialists, tried multiple medications, or been told "your labs are normal" even though you don't feel well…you're not alone.

More adults than ever are living with multiple chronic conditions like IBS, autoimmune disease, fatigue, mood symptoms, and metabolic issues, and the traditional 10–15 minute visit often isn't designed to untangle the complexity that lies underneath these conditions.

It's not that conventional medicine is "bad" — it does an excellent job with emergencies, infections, and acute care. Still, it can fall short when your symptoms are chronic, confusing, and interconnected.

After years of struggling with symptoms with no clear answers, many people turn to the internet for answers to questions like:

  • "Should I see a functional medicine doctor?"
  • "Is functional medicine legit?"
  • "How can functional medicine help my gut, autoimmunity, or fatigue?"

If that's how you found yourself here, this article will help you understand what functional medicine is, who it's a good fit for, and what to expect if you work with a functional medicine practitioner at Avicenna Functional Medicine.

What is functional medicine?

In simpler terms, functional medicine is a type of personalized healthcare that focuses on identifying the root causes of your symptoms and health conditions.

Functional medicine practitioners look at how all of your body systems interact and overlap, identify areas of imbalance or insufficiency, and use personalized nutrition, lifestyle, and targeted therapies to support a healthier balance.

Instead of asking only "What diagnosis fits these symptoms?" functional medicine adds a second layer:

"Why is your body creating these symptoms in the first place?"

The best functional medicine providers understand that your symptoms make sense in the context of what's going on in your body, and that by addressing core imbalances, symptoms may improve.

What does a visit include?

Here's what to expect from a functional medicine session:

  • A detailed life history (often going back to birth).
  • Questions about your gut, skin, mood, sleep, stress, and environment.
  • A timeline of your health events (infections, injuries, medications, major life stressors).
  • Functional lab work that looks for patterns — not just "normal vs. abnormal."

At Avicenna Functional Medicine, there's a special focus on the gut microbiome, chronic inflammation, and how they connect to conditions like IBS, IBD, SIBO, autoimmunity, and unexplained systemic symptoms. Research continues to show that the gut microbiome plays a central role in the development and progression of many chronic inflammatory diseases.

How is it different from a typical doctor's visit?

A common question patients often wonder is, "What does a functional medicine doctor do differently than a traditional doctor?" Here are a few key distinctions:

More time and a broader lens

Functional medicine visits are longer. You'll spend 30 minutes to an hour with your primary functional provider instead of the average 8 minutes common to traditional doctor visits. The extra time is used to understand your story, connect the dots between symptoms, and explore diet, stress, sleep, trauma history, toxins, and microbiome health, and outline your personalized care plan.

Systems-based, not organ-based

Instead of separating you into "gut," "skin," "hormones," or "mental health," a functional medicine practitioner looks at how all of these are linked. For example: gut dysbiosis, immune activation, skin flares, brain fog, and fatigue are often connected rather than separate problems.

A partnership, not top-down care

You're not a passive recipient of instructions. Functional medicine is inherently patient-centered, emphasizing shared decision-making and co-created treatment plans. This kind of person-centered care is associated with better satisfaction and improved well-being in people with chronic conditions.

Who should consider a functional medicine practitioner?

You might be a good candidate for functional medicine if:

  • You've seen multiple doctors and still don't have clear answers.
  • You have numerous chronic symptoms (gut issues, fatigue, joint pain, skin issues, mood symptoms) that seem connected but haven't been treated as such.
  • You suspect your gut microbiome, diet, or environment might be playing a role.
  • You want to use nutrition, lifestyle, and root-cause strategies alongside appropriate medications.
  • You're willing to be an active participant in your own care.

Functional medicine is especially helpful for:

  • Gut and microbiome issues: IBS, bloating, constipation/diarrhea, SIBO, IBD support.
  • Autoimmune and inflammatory conditions: joint pain, skin conditions, thyroid autoimmunity, etc.
  • Chronic fatigue and "mystery symptoms" that haven't responded well to conventional care.
  • Prevention and optimization, if you have a strong family history of chronic disease and want to be proactive.
  • POTS, dysautonomia, and long-COVID syndrome symptoms that are disrupting your life.

Is functional medicine evidence-based?

This is an important and very reasonable question.

Functional medicine is not a single protocol or brand-name treatment — it's a framework that combines conventional diagnostics and lab work, established nutrition and lifestyle sciences, and up-to-date research on the gut microbiome, immune system, metabolic health, and environmental factors.

The quality of functional medicine depends heavily on who practices it and how grounded they are in current, peer-reviewed research. At Avicenna Functional Medicine, our approach is rooted in conventional medical training and physiology. Our personalized strategy and interventions are informed by current literature on the gut microbiome and chronic inflammatory disease, and guided by person-centered care principles to ensure everything we offer for support is doable and achievable for each individual.

The goal is not to replace conventional medicine, but to complement it and fill in the gaps — especially when you're dealing with long-standing issues that haven't responded to a symptom-only approach.

Common questions before booking

Will it replace my primary care doctor?

No. You still need a primary care provider for:

  • Routine physicals, checkups, and medication guidance/prescription.
  • Acute issues (infections, injuries).
  • Screenings, exams, and vaccines.
  • Urgent and emergency care.

Functional medicine is ideal as a partner in your care, especially for chronic, complex, or microbiome-related conditions.

What tests might be ordered?

Testing is individualized and based on symptoms and concerns, but may include:

  • Standard blood work (inflammation, nutrients, blood sugar, thyroid, etc.).
  • Advanced stool testing to evaluate gut microbiome diversity, digestion, and markers of gut inflammation.
  • Urinary toxin testing.
  • Select hormone, metabolic, or immune markers depending on your history.

The goal isn't to run every test possible, but to choose tests that meaningfully influence and guide your treatment plan.

How long until I feel better?

Each case is unique and depends strongly on factors such as:

  • How long you've had symptoms.
  • How many systems are involved (gut, immune, hormones, nervous system).
  • How ready you are to make nutrition and lifestyle changes.

Most chronic conditions don't change overnight, but some people begin to notice shifts in digestion, energy, or sleep when the root causes are addressed and the microbiome is supported. How quickly anything changes varies from person to person.

Functional medicine for gut and microbiome issues

Gut health and microbiome balance are often a key focus at Avicenna Functional Medicine. Functional medicine can help you:

  • Identify food patterns and triggers contributing to bloating, pain, or altered bowel habits.
  • Understand how past infections, antibiotics, or stress may have changed your microbiome.
  • Use targeted nutrition, lifestyle, and evidence-informed supplementation strategies to support microbial diversity.

Emerging research continues to show that the gut microbiome influences chronic inflammatory conditions throughout the body, from the intestines to the joints, skin, and beyond. Functional medicine translates that science into practical, personalized care plans.

What to expect with Avicenna

If you decide to explore functional medicine with Avicenna Functional Medicine, you can expect:

  • Virtual visits — care from home, ideal if symptoms make travel difficult.
  • A comprehensive intake that explores your history, triggers, and goals.
  • A focus on root-cause analysis and personalized functional testing strategies.
  • A personalized plan that can include nutrition, lifestyle shifts, microbiome support, and collaboration with your other providers.

You're not just given a quick diagnosis and prescription; you're invited into a therapeutic partnership designed to uncover why your symptoms are happening and what steps may help you work toward better balance.

Functional medicine and integrative medicine

Integrative medicine is an approach that combines conventional medical practices with alternative therapies, emphasizing a holistic view of health. This approach recognizes that health is not merely the absence of disease but a state of physical, emotional, and spiritual well-being.

Practitioners of integrative medicine assess patients in their entirety, considering lifestyle, environmental factors, and psychological health alongside traditional diagnoses and treatments. By incorporating a variety of therapeutic modalities — including nutrition, acupuncture, herbal medicine, and mindfulness practices — integrative medicine aims to enhance the overall healing process and improve the patient's quality of life.

Integrative medicine also fosters a collaborative relationship between the practitioner and the patient, encouraging active participation in health decisions. This model empowers individuals to take charge of their health journey by addressing the root causes of disorders rather than just alleviating symptoms.

So, should you see a functional medicine practitioner?

If you feel like you've tried "everything" and still don't feel like yourself, have gut issues, chronic inflammation, or autoimmune symptoms that conventional care hasn't fully addressed, or you want a practitioner who will listen deeply, connect the dots, and work with you on a root-cause plan — then functional medicine may be precisely the next step you've been looking for.
