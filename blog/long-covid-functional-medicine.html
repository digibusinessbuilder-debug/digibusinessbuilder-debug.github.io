CHRONIC ILLNESS RECOVERY

Long COVID: A Functional Medicine Approach

Jordan Casey, MS-CNFM · Master of Science in Clinical Nutrition and Functional Medicine, University of Western States

Medically reviewed by Fawad Ahmed, MD on September 28, 2025

Published September 28, 2025 · Updated October 15, 2026

Long COVID is a multisystem syndrome, not a simple post-viral fatigue. Here's what drives it — metabolic disruption, microclots, mast cell activation, dysautonomia, immune dysregulation, and mitochondrial injury — and a functional medicine framework for recovery.

Just 5 years ago, the world experienced one of the most unprecedented global events that many of us alive today have ever experienced. A highly contagious and lethal novel virus quickly spread across the globe.

As the world entered a state of emergency and shut down for several months with mandatory shelter-in-place orders, the focus at the time was on controlling the spread and supporting those infected in critical condition as best as possible. No one expected the aftermath that was to come as a result of a COVID-19 infection.

In 2021, the WHO formally recognized Long COVID as a real health condition that consisted of "symptoms that persist for at least two months, generally three months after the initial infection."

Long COVID affects millions worldwide — extending far beyond temporary fatigue to a multifaceted syndrome involving nearly every body system. The manifestation of the condition is different depending on the individual. It appears to possess a unique ability to exploit areas of vulnerability, such as pre-existing conditions, genetic predispositions, or unhealthy dietary/lifestyle habits.

Symptoms of long COVID

General and body-wide

  • Persistent fatigue
  • Post-exertional malaise (PEM)
  • Recurring or low-grade fevers
  • Joint and muscle pain

Neurological

  • Brain fog
  • Headaches
  • Sleep problems (insomnia, poor sleep quality)
  • Dizziness and vertigo
  • Dysautonomia (e.g., POTS)
  • Altered or lost smell and taste
  • Pins-and-needles feelings
  • Vision problems

Heart and lungs

  • Shortness of breath
  • Chronic cough
  • Chest pain
  • Heart palpitations
  • Increased risk of blood clots

Digestive

  • Persistent stomach pain
  • Constipation or diarrhea
  • Nausea and appetite changes

Mental health

  • Anxiety and depression
  • Mood swings
  • Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)

Other symptoms

  • Skin rashes
  • Hair loss
  • Irregular periods
  • Sexual dysfunction

In this article, we explore how Long COVID can disrupt metabolism, immune balance, cardiovascular health, and more — and how a functional medicine framework can help you work toward renewed vitality. What's become clear is that Long COVID is not just a continuation of an acute illness; it's a multisystem syndrome demanding a root-cause approach.

What causes long COVID?

We are still too early in the aftermath of COVID-19 to fully understand the mechanisms driving Long COVID. However, several hypotheses have been presented, including:

  • Ongoing viral activation in tissues
  • Immune system dysregulation and potential influence of other chronic viral loads, such as the herpes virus-6 or Epstein-Barr virus
  • Changes to the gut microbiome
  • Autoimmune activation by a process known as "molecular mimicry," where native tissues are attacked because they are similar in structure to viral proteins the immune system is fighting against
  • Blood clotting issues due to the formation of "microclots" and endothelial dysfunction
  • Disrupted communication between the brain and body, and dysregulated vagus nerve signalling

A comprehensive review in Nature Reviews Microbiology describes these potential causes of Long COVID as overlapping mechanisms. Meaning it likely is not just "one thing" or a singular cause — instead, it's a combination of these factors that creates the "perfect storm" that results in Long COVID.

Supporting recovery requires more than mere symptom relief. It calls for a comprehensive functional approach that addresses the root cause(s) of symptoms by supporting the vulnerable pathways underlying the pathology. This personalized and curated approach identifies the key areas that need support, with the goal of helping you reclaim your vitality.

How long COVID disrupts body systems

1. Metabolic disruption

Studies show that COVID often causes significant metabolic shifts. Most notably, HDL (your "good" cholesterol) decreases and LDL increases. Several studies have noted an increase in insulin resistance and inflammation markers in Long COVID. One study found that people with more severe forms of Long COVID had more significant reductions in HDL, higher ferritin, and greater insulin resistance.

Each of these conditions, both individually and collectively, is a risk factor for cardiovascular disease, diabetes, liver disease, and metabolic syndrome.

Moreover, one review of Long COVID research suggests a bidirectional relationship between metabolic health and COVID outcomes. Pre-existing metabolic issues increase Long COVID risk, and Long COVID itself worsens metabolic profiles even further.

Many of these metabolic disturbances are driven by an influx of molecules called cytokines that create an inflammatory cascade, leading to more metabolic chaos. As a result of the chronic inflammation, iron metabolism is also disrupted. Elevated ferritin levels are another inflammatory marker of Long COVID, suggestive of iron dysregulation, which contributes further to inflammation and impaired energy production.

Why this matters: Metabolic dysfunction often manifests as persistent fatigue, slowed recovery, and vulnerable immunity. Low cellular energy production fuels systemic inflammation, slows recovery, increases risk for cardiovascular and neurodegenerative trajectories, and contributes to persistent fatigue.

Approaches for metabolic disruption

  • Anti-inflammatory, fiber-rich, Mediterranean-style diets support metabolic health.
  • Low-carb and low-saturated-fat intake help improve lipid parameters and insulin resistance.
  • Omega-3s (ideally from krill oil), vitamin D, and vitamin A (as retinol) bolster immune regulation to reduce inflammation and oxidative stress.
  • NAC and glycine boost natural antioxidant production.
  • Adaptogens and choline (rich in krill oil) also support mitochondrial energy production.

2. Microclots and blood vessel changes

Researchers have identified a type of "microclotting" that occurs in the blood as a result of COVID. Amyloid-type fibrin (fibrinaloid) microclots are tiny protein formations that are resistant to the normal clot-busting functions of the body, called fibrinolysis. These protein aggregates in the blood hinder efficient blood flow, subsequently reducing oxygen delivery to tissues.

Researchers studying amyloid fibrins in Long COVID patients have found that proteolytic enzymes are emerging as an effective natural treatment to aid in breaking down these fibrins. While findings on amyloid fibrils as the cause of Long COVID are still debated, it is known that they are not unique to Long COVID. It may be more likely that endothelial dysfunction and microvascular injury are the likely culprits behind pervasive symptoms like fatigue and brain fog.

Mechanistic reviews highlight microvascular effects and reduced tissue blood supply as contributors to a wide range of Long COVID symptoms — from brain fog to exercise intolerance.

Why this matters: When microcirculation is impaired, cellular metabolism stalls. Amyloid fibrins may compromise cellular oxygenation and endothelial integrity, perpetuating fatigue, cognitive difficulties, and autonomic imbalance. Functional protocols must incorporate endothelial support, gentle fibrinolytic strategies, and oxygen-optimizing habits.

Approaches for microclots

  • Endothelial-supportive nutrients like lipoic acid, polyphenols (from plants), and nitric oxide (NO) may be effective.
  • Gentle circulation-enhancing activity like walking and compression therapy may be beneficial.
  • Phased anticoagulant or fibrinolytic protocols with practitioner oversight, and micronutrients like glutathione to support vascular protection.

3. Mast cell activation and histamine intolerance

Long COVID often triggers mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS), a condition where mast cells release inflammatory mediators — histamine, cytokines, proteases — that affect multiple organs.

Mast cells normally play a role in allergic responses, but when overactive, they release excess histamine and other inflammatory chemicals. This can cause flushing, headaches, rashes, digestive upset, brain fog, and a racing heart.

Studies show that antihistamine medications significantly improve cardiovascular and systemic symptoms in Long COVID patients, speaking to the role of regulating histamine and mast cells as a treatment strategy.

Why this matters: When mast cells stay on "high alert," histamine surges through the bloodstream, keeping your body in a state of inflammation. This can intensify fatigue, trigger food sensitivities, and exacerbate nervous system symptoms such as dizziness or palpitations. MCAS is a common comorbidity seen with POTS and dysautonomia.

Approaches for mast cell activation

  • Follow a low-histamine diet, limiting fermented foods, alcohol, aged cheeses, canned meats, and leftovers.
  • Use targeted supports such as a DAO enzyme supplement, natural vitamin C (not ascorbic acid), and quercetin to stabilize mast cells and calm inflammation.
  • Address gut health, because a balanced microbiome helps regulate histamine breakdown. It's key to work with a functional medicine specialist who understands the complexities of these conditions — foods that support the gut are often high-histamine foods, but gut health is an essential root cause to address.
  • A functional medicine provider who specializes in Long COVID and dysautonomia will help you effectively navigate these challenges.

4. Dysautonomia, POTS and neurological strain

Many people with Long COVID develop dysautonomia — dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system, which regulates heart rate, blood pressure, and digestion.

A common presentation is Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS), characterized by a rapid heart rate and a drop in blood pressure when standing. Studies suggest that up to two-thirds of Long COVID patients show signs of POTS or small-fiber neuropathy.

Why this matters: Dysautonomia can make everyday activities, such as standing, walking, and even eating, a challenge. Unstable heart rate and blood pressure reduce blood flow to the brain, causing dizziness, fatigue, and "brain fog," and can delay recovery of every other system. Gastrointestinal issues and MCAS are common comorbidities seen with dysautonomia. It's unclear which causes the other; it's likely a "perfect storm" of imbalances that allow these conditions to manifest.

Approaches for dysautonomia

  • Maintain adequate hydration and electrolytes to support blood volume.
  • Use compression garments or gentle resistance exercise to help blood return to the heart.
  • Support nerve signaling with choline-rich foods and omega-3s — krill oil is a good source of both.
  • Practice vagus-nerve exercises (slow breathing, humming, gargling) to improve nervous-system balance.

5. Immune dysregulation and autoimmunity

Research reveals that Long COVID often involves ongoing immune activation, including elevated cytokines and the formation of autoantibodies — proteins that mistakenly attack the body's own tissues.

Some patients also experience reactivation of latent viruses like Epstein-Barr virus, which can worsen fatigue and trigger autoimmune reactions. Mast cell and endothelial dysfunction caused by amyloid fibrils and persistent inflammation amplify this cycle, laying the groundwork for the development of autoimmune disease.

Why this matters: A constantly "revved up" immune system can lead to chronic inflammation, new food sensitivities, joint pain, and the onset of autoimmune conditions such as autoimmune thyroiditis, lupus, and rheumatoid-like symptoms. Elevated ANA antibodies are often seen in many Long COVID patients.

Approaches for immune dysregulation

  • Boost antioxidant defenses with NAC + glycine to raise glutathione levels. Support immune modulation with vitamin D, cod liver oil, and antiviral herbs and mushrooms.
  • Strengthen gut integrity and microbiome diversity through a high-fiber, polyphenol-rich diet to reduce immune triggers.
  • Incorporate stress-management practices — breathwork, meditation, or gentle yoga — to calm inflammatory pathways.

6. Mitochondrial injury and low energy

Your mitochondria are the tiny "power plants" inside every cell. Network analyses show that COVID-19 can disrupt mitochondrial function, creating an energy deficit that drives persistent fatigue and exercise intolerance.

When mitochondria can't produce enough ATP (cellular energy), every system in your body — immune, cardiovascular, and neurological — struggles to recover. Recent research using quantum mechanics has analyzed specific systems and pathways disrupted by COVID, highlighting mitochondrial dysfunction as a central mechanism in Long COVID, along with inflammatory signalling and blood clotting issues such as amyloid microfibrils.

Why mitochondrial damage matters

Low cellular energy means slower healing, poor exercise tolerance, and constant exhaustion — even after mild activity. Without healthy mitochondria, the body can't efficiently repair tissues, clear lingering inflammation, or effectively suppress viral invaders like COVID.

Approaches that support mitochondria

  • Use adaptogens (rhodiola, ashwagandha) to improve energy resilience.
  • Provide key cofactors such as B-vitamins, NAD+ precursors, and choline to feed the energy cycle and support a robust mitochondrial membrane. It requires energy to make energy; D-ribose may be a beneficial supplement to initiate sufficient energy metabolism and support recovery.
  • Support antioxidant defenses with glutathione (NAC + glycine, or IV therapy when appropriate).
  • Practice activity pacing — alternating movement with rest — to avoid energy crashes and allow gradual recovery.
  • Cold exposure, such as safe cold plunges or cold showers, helps increase mitochondrial numbers, though this should be reserved as a second- or third-phase approach with a qualified functional medicine practitioner.

A functional medicine care plan for long COVID

Diet and lifestyle foundations

  • Anti-inflammatory, nutrient-dense diet: emphasize a colorful, anti-inflammatory diet rich in vegetables, fruits, clean proteins, omega-3 fats, and gut-supportive foods.
  • Balance blood sugar with regular, nutrient-dense meals that are low in simple sugars/carbs and rich in fiber.
  • Low-histamine options for MCAS: limit fermented foods, aged cheeses, and alcohol.
  • Movement for circulation: gentle strength, yoga, rebounding, and walks to support microvascular and autonomic recovery.

Targeted nutrients and supplements

(always under practitioner guidance — this is essential for determining which areas warrant foundational support given your unique symptom presentation)

  • A mast-cell-stabilizing, histamine-supporting formula combined with krill oil for omega-3s and choline to support nerve and immune balance.
  • NAC + glycine to boost glutathione and reduce oxidative stress.
  • Garlic and MSM for healthy immune modulation and autoantibody balance.
  • Targeted iron- and immune-support nutrients, cod liver oil, and vitamin D to help rebalance iron metabolism and support viral clearance and immune balance.
  • Adaptogens, B-vitamins, NAD+ precursors, and choline to support mitochondrial and nervous system repair.
  • IV glutathione and vitamin C (when appropriate) for enhanced antioxidant and immune support in pronounced oxidative stress.

Recovery and repair practices

  • Consistent sleep hygiene for immune and mitochondrial repair.
  • Stress regulation through breathwork, meditation, or limbic retraining.
  • Adequate hydration and minerals, specifically salt, are vital for those with POTS or dysautonomia.
  • Gentle autonomic retraining: light vagal nerve stimulation, HRV tools, and proprioceptive training protocols.

What recovery can look like

Timelines vary widely from person to person, depending on severity and consistency with a personalized plan.

Healing is rarely linear — allow for peaks and plateaus with patience, consistency, and adjustments. A skilled functional medicine practitioner specializing in Long COVID will be able to guide you through these adaptations to help you stay on a clear path to recovery.

The path forward

Long COVID isn't "just in your head," and it's not a simple post-viral fatigue. It's a multi-system condition that calls for a personalized plan that aims to calm the immune system, improve energy, and support repair of the nervous and vascular systems.

Functional medicine offers a roadmap that aims to support recovery by addressing the underlying root causes — not just masking symptoms — with evidence-backed diet, nutrients, lifestyle, and compassionate pacing.
