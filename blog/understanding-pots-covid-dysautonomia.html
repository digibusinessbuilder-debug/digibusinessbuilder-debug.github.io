Understanding POTS: The Hidden Connection Between COVID and Your Nervous System

Jordan Casey, MS-CNFM · Master of Science in Clinical Nutrition and Functional Medicine, University of Western States

Medically reviewed by Fawad Ahmed, MD on October 13, 2025

Published October 13, 2025 · Updated October 15, 2026

Learn how POTS—often triggered after COVID—disrupts the autonomic nervous system, and explore its symptoms, subtypes, testing options, MCAS links, and functional strategies for relief.

Book a Consultation

If you find yourself feeling dizzy, lightheaded, with a racing heart every time you stand up, and you genuinely feel unwell, but your labs look fine, and you're told "nothing's wrong," then we need to talk.

Something is definitely wrong!

These symptoms may be signs of an invisible condition that can dramatically impact your quality of life. They are often undetected on the outside, while on the inside, you feel entirely out of balance.

These symptoms are collectively recognized as "dysautonomia," which means there's an imbalance in your autonomic nervous system function—the system that regulates your heart rate, breathing, digestion, and other bodily functions that work without conscious effort.

Dysautonomia comes in various forms. One common symptom presentation would be classified as Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS).

This condition affects millions of people worldwide and is becoming increasingly prevalent, particularly following the COVID-19 pandemic.

What is POTS?

Simply put, POTS is a form of autonomic dysfunction that causes your heart rate to increase excessively when you transition from lying down to a standing position.

The American Autonomic Society and the Heart Rhythm Society have established clinical criteria to formally diagnose POTS. They include:

  • Frequent symptoms that occur with standing, such as lightheadedness, palpitations, tremulousness, generalized weakness, blurred vision, exercise intolerance, and fatigue
  • An increase in heart rate of ≥30 bpm when moving from a recumbent [lying] to a standing position held for more than 30 seconds (or ≥40 bpm in individuals 12 to 19 years of age)
  • The absence of orthostatic hypotension (>20 mm Hg drop in systolic blood pressure)

Other potential causes of these symptoms should be ruled out and may include dehydration, anemia, and hyperthyroidism.

Growing awareness of POTS

Increasing awareness of POTS in the medical community should be considered a critical initiative.

Key statistics

A survey of POTS patients from 2019 found that most POTS patients were misdiagnosed, and the average time to a proper POTS diagnosis is 2 years, with some taking up to 72 months. Several different physicians were also seen before arriving at the appropriate diagnosis.

There is a strong female dominance in POTS (about 80% of POTS cases are women), and onset/diagnosis usually occurs between 15-25 years of age, suggesting a potential influence of female hormones.

Upwards of 25% of POTS patients experience disability as a result of POTS, with it affecting their ability to attend work or school.

Ever since the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, rates of POTS have dramatically increased by nearly fourfold. Pre-pandemic, POTS rates were 1.42 cases per 100,000 people; after 2020, it has risen to 20.3/100,000, which is likely an underestimation still, given the frequency of misdiagnosis and lengthy diagnosis time.

What causes POTS?

POTS is a complex and complicated condition that usually manifests as a result of several dysfunctional systems within the body rather than a sole singular cause.

This is where working with a POTS-specialized functional medicine provider, along with the right medical care team, can make a difference in working toward symptom relief and avoiding further misdiagnosis.

Cumulatively, POTS is a dysregulated autonomic nervous system (ANS) response. When you stand from a seated or lying position, your nervous system should be automatically aware of this, and specific physiological changes should occur:

  • The ANS detects a microsecond shift in blood pressure as you stand
  • Your ANS notices this and causes blood vessels to contract, squeezing blood upward to your brain, as it's now working harder against gravity
  • Your heart rate should mildly increase to help nudge the blood along

With POTS, the ANS response is weakened or delayed, which causes blood pooling in the legs and feet and reduced blood flow to the brain. The low blood volume in the brain is the cause of dizziness, lightheadedness/faintness, and blurred vision symptoms common to POTS.

The rapid heartbeat experienced by POTS patients is a compensatory mechanism of the body. The heart works overtime in attempts to get blood moving upward.

Types of POTS

Understanding the underlying cause of your POTS symptoms is crucial for effective treatment. With so much overlap and multifactorial contributions to ANS dysregulation, identifying core contributing factors in your unique case is vital.

Functional medicine assesses the timeline of each individual, mapping out major events that have happened throughout your life to identify potential triggers and mediators, in addition to potential predisposing factors such as genetic predispositions.

Medical research has identified several distinct forms of this condition, each with different underlying mechanisms. Combining these conditions with a functional lifestyle assessment enables the development of a precise care plan that aims to improve ANS function at its core.

Hypovolemic POTS

Occurs when the body's blood volume is abnormally low. It results in less blood being pumped to the heart due to the low volume, causing the heart to beat faster. This can be caused by an electrolyte imbalance in the ratio of salt and water, or by dysfunction of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), which regulates blood volume and blood pressure. A 24-hour urine test can help identify this form of POTS: when urine sodium levels are less than 100 mmol/L, this indicates problems with the body's salt and fluid balance.

Hyperadrenergic POTS

The result of an overactive sympathetic nervous system (the fight-or-flight response). In rare cases, this can be caused by a mutation in the norepinephrine transporter (NET) that results in high levels of norepinephrine, characterized by standing plasma norepinephrine levels of 600 pg/mL or higher. Patients with this form often experience additional symptoms like increased systolic blood pressure, palpitations, anxiety, and tremors, in addition to the characteristic tachycardia.

Neuropathic POTS

Opposite of hyperadrenergic POTS, neuropathic POTS is caused by insufficient epinephrine release of peripheral nerves of the ANS, particularly in the small fiber nerves of the lower extremities. This condition may also arise due to partial neuropathy caused by nerve damage, autoimmune disorders, or viral infections. Early POTS research from the Mayo Clinic found that at least half of POTS patients showed evidence of a neuropathic pattern, with many having abnormal sweat responses.

The POTS–MCAS connection

An increasingly recognized association exists between POTS and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS). Research has found that 66% of POTS patients who also experienced additional symptoms such as migraines, allergies, rashes, and GI complaints showed laboratory findings consistent with mast cell activation syndrome.

Female patients with both POTS and MCAS often experience episodes of skin flushing, shortness of breath, headaches, lightheadedness, excessive urination, and gastrointestinal symptoms triggered by standing, exercise, menstrual cycles, meals, and other factors.

This connection is of particular importance because mast cell activation triggers histamine release, which is known to affect blood vessel function, reducing their responsiveness to ANS signals, which worsens POTS symptoms.

Environmental factors can play a significant role in triggering MCAS, with mold exposure being a notable concern. Mold exposure can trigger mast cell activation in susceptible individuals, potentially worsening both POTS and MCAS symptoms. Removing oneself from moldy environments may be necessary for some patients to achieve symptom improvement.

Science has also observed a strong connection between COVID-19 infection and MCAS. While research is still early, the overlap of symptoms and prevalence justifies assessing and addressing long-COVID in POTS cases with additional MCAS symptoms.

A functional medicine approach to POTS

What makes POTS so difficult to address by conventional medical standards is that it's a multi-organ-systems issue, rather than being isolated to one body system. The symptoms of POTS show up as cardiovascular, which you might see a cardiologist for. But the origin may be neurological, immunological, or both.

The goal of functional medicine is to identify and address the root causes of POTS, rather than merely addressing the symptoms. However, managing symptoms should always be a key focus of the functional care plan, given their debilitating nature.

Successful treatment must be tailored to the specific type and underlying mechanisms of each person's POTS presentation.

Finding the underlying cause

The first step in functional treatment is comprehensive testing to determine which type of POTS you have. This may include:

Tests that help

  • 24-hour urine sodium testing to assess for hypovolemic POTS
  • Standing and supine catecholamine levels to identify hyperadrenergic forms
  • Autonomic function testing to evaluate for neuropathic POTS
  • Genetic and connective tissue assessments
  • Exploring the connection with MCAS when suspected

Electrolytes and hydration

Proper electrolyte balance is fundamental for all POTS patients. This goes beyond simply drinking more water—it requires understanding the intricate balance of sodium, potassium, and magnesium that supports proper blood volume and cardiovascular function.

For patients with hypovolemic POTS, upwards of 7g of sodium per day may be necessary, but it is essential to consult with a healthcare provider who understands the complexity of POTS as well as other conditions you may have before ingesting this much sodium.

Low-histamine diet and antihistamine support

For patients with the POTS-MCAS connection, dietary modifications can be crucial. A low-histamine diet eliminates foods that are high in histamine or trigger histamine release, potentially reducing mast cell activation and associated symptoms.

Natural anti-histamine supplements that may help include:

  • DAO (Diamine Oxidase) enzyme to break down dietary histamine
  • Nettle leaf extract for natural antihistamine effects
  • Quercetin as a mast cell stabilizer
  • Bromelain for anti-inflammatory support
  • Ursolic acid for mast cell stabilization
  • Tulsi (holy basil) for adaptive support

It's important to note that many people with histamine issues cannot tolerate ascorbic acid (vitamin C), as it can trigger histamine release in sensitive individuals. Additionally, supplement fillers and flow agents commonly used in manufacturing can also trigger reactions, so choosing clean, additive-free supplements is essential.

Exercise and proprioceptive training

Proprioceptive training is a form of exercise that improves your body's awareness of its position in space. The basis of this exercise holds potential for improving the mechanisms involved in POTS.

While research is limited on the effects of proprioceptive training specifically in POTS populations, healthcare professionals who work with individuals with POTS have developed exercise protocols with impressive anecdotal results for POTS.

The CHOP Protocol and Levine Protocol are exercise routines that were developed by medical doctors who work with POTS to improve exercise tolerance. They are specifically tailored to support this population.

Exercise is essential for POTS patients, and especially for those with connective tissue disease influences, but the very nature of POTS makes exercise feel impossible. These protocols can help train the body and nervous system to tolerate exercise (and subsequently, regular, daily movements).

Nervous system retraining

Because POTS is a dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system, many patients find relief in nervous system retraining programs, such as cognitive behavioral therapy and limbic system retraining programs.

  • Studies have found that calming the hypervigilance reaction of the nervous system helps improve symptoms associated with MCAS/immunologically-mediated POTS
  • ECG training for improving heart rate variability was found to improve POTS symptoms in a small pilot study
  • Vagus nerve stimulation via the ear may be a promising therapy for POTS as well. Researchers noted a decrease in heart rate upon standing by 15 BPM in people who receive vagal stimulation

Other supportive approaches

The best approach is holistic and includes reducing exposure to triggers (such as histamine), preventing symptoms (like staying adequately hydrated with electrolytes), helping the nervous system adapt, and reducing physiological stress.

These strategies include factors such as:

  • Addressing sleep disorders and optimizing sleep quality
  • Managing stress and supporting adrenal function
  • Correcting nutritional deficiencies that affect autonomic function
  • Supporting mitochondrial function for cellular energy production
  • Maintaining muscle strength with resistance training to strengthen connective tissue

The path forward

POTS is a complex condition that requires comprehensive evaluation and individualized treatment. While the journey to wellness can be challenging, understanding your specific type of POTS and addressing its root causes through functional medicine approaches may help improve symptoms and quality of life.

The growing recognition of post-COVID POTS and the connections between POTS, connective tissue disorders, and mast cell activation has opened new avenues for treatment. As research continues to evolve, patients have more hope than ever for effective, root-cause-focused care.

If you're struggling with unexplained heart rate increases when standing, chronic fatigue, dizziness, or other symptoms that might suggest POTS, don't wait. The sooner you can identify the underlying causes and begin appropriate treatment, the sooner you can begin working toward better health and vitality.
